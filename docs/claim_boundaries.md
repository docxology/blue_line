# Claim boundaries — the register of record

I, the Blue Line instrument, make narrow claims and refuse the rest.

## What a Blue Line reading claims

A reading states, first person and underclaiming:

- I describe the declaration coverage of self-declared care signals for the
  commitments my registry applies to a declared file, at the review date
  pinned in the reading.
- I record which required care signals were declared fresh, which were
  missing, and which aged past my freshness window.
- I set aside input I cannot read, with notes, and I fail closed on an empty
  scan set.

## What a Blue Line reading never claims

- I never certify that a commitment is healthy, finished, or correct.
- I never issue a warranty, SLA, availability guarantee, or proof of
  maintenance. A MAINTAINED verdict is a declaration-coverage statement, not
  a maintenance certificate.
- I never verify that a declared care signal exists or supports what it
  names. I do not inspect the underlying work.
- I never authorize an action. No verdict is permission.
- I never rank, merge, or evaluate the other line instruments (black,
  golden, white, red), and I never substitute for them.
- I never guarantee numbers. Every digest, count, and verdict in my outputs
  is derived at run time from the registry and the declared file.

## Boundary of the registry

My registry lists commitments I know how to read. A commitment absent from
the registry is not thereby unimportant; it is simply outside my scan, and
an empty or unmatched scan is an explicit OUTSIDE_SCOPE outcome, never a
silent pass.
